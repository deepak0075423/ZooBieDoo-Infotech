About us

A consultancy built to be told the truth by, and to tell it back.

We started in 2013 with four engineers and one belief that has not changed: most technology failures are communication failures wearing technical clothing. We built the firm around fixing that.

Our story

Twelve years, four offices, and a deliberate ceiling on how fast we grow.

Zoobiedoo Infotech was founded by four engineers who had spent the previous decade inside enterprise IT programmes and had watched the same failure repeat: capable people delivering the wrong thing competently, because nobody had been willing to say early enough that the plan was wrong.

Our first engagement was a settlement reconciliation system for a mid-tier broker. It went live eleven weeks late, and the post-mortem we wrote for that client — unprompted, and unflattering to us — is still the document we hand new joiners in their first week.

We have grown to 240 people across Noida, London, Singapore and Austin. We have also turned down work every year we have been in business, because the alternative is staffing engagements with people who are not ready and calling it capacity.

We are privately held, have taken no external investment, and have no revenue target that requires us to recommend work a client does not need.

What we hold to

Four commitments, and the cost of each one

Values are only meaningful when they cost something. Here is what these cost us.

Say the difficult thing early

Bad news does not improve with age. Our delivery leads are measured partly on how quickly problems reach the people who can act on them.

Recommend against ourselves

We have advised clients to buy rather than build, to keep their existing supplier, and not to proceed. It costs revenue and it is why the relationships last.

Write it down

Architecture decisions, trade-offs, incident reviews and handover plans exist as documents. Tribal knowledge is a risk we deliberately price out.

Leave clients independent

Every engagement has a defined exit. When a client takes work in-house because we prepared them to, we count it as the engagement working.

How we are structured

Practices, not a resource pool

Every engineer and designer belongs to a practice led by a principal who is still hands-on. Practices own their standards, their hiring bar and their technical direction; delivery teams are assembled across practices for each engagement.

It is a more expensive model than a general resource pool. It is also why a mobile engineer here is a mobile specialist rather than whoever was available, and why our technical decisions are consistent between engagements.

  • A named delivery lead accountable for each engagement end to end
  • Principals allocated to delivery, not only to sales
  • Peer review across practices on architecture decisions
  • Published progression frameworks for every discipline
  • No sub-contracting without written client agreement
