Drupal Commerce, WooCommerce or Shopify: choosing an e-commerce platform

The right e-commerce platform depends on how unusual your catalogue and checkout rules are, and how much of the operation is bespoke.

ZooBieDoo Infotech Engineering Team 04 June 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Let the unusual requirements decide, not the feature comparison tables.
  • Shopify for speed to launch; WooCommerce for content-led; Drupal Commerce for complexity.
  • Integration with stock, pricing and finance is usually the real work.
  • Model total cost over three years, not headline pricing.

Start with the awkward requirements

Every e-commerce platform handles a simple catalogue and a card payment. The decision is made by the requirements that are unusual for you: complex product configuration, contract pricing per customer, mixed physical and digital fulfilment, unusual tax treatment, or deep integration with an existing ERP.

List those first. They eliminate more options than the feature comparison tables do.

Where each one fits

  • Shopify — fastest to launch, strong hosted operations, and the right answer when your model fits its assumptions. Costs rise with bespoke behaviour.
  • WooCommerce — sensible when the site is content-led and commerce is a component rather than the whole business.
  • Drupal Commerce — strongest where the product model is genuinely complex, pricing rules are unusual, or commerce must sit inside a larger content and workflow platform.

The integration question

For most established businesses the real work is not the storefront, it is the connection to stock, pricing, fulfilment and finance systems. A platform that makes that integration awkward will cost far more over three years than one with a slightly weaker storefront.

Ask how each candidate handles asynchronous updates from your systems of record, and what happens when they disagree.

Total cost over three years

Compare licence or transaction fees, hosting, the cost of the integrations you actually need, and the cost of maintaining them. A platform with no licence fee but three bespoke integrations to maintain is not the cheap option.

Model it explicitly rather than comparing headline prices.

Questions to ask before you choose

A structured evaluation beats an opinion, and the questions that separate the candidates are rarely the ones on the vendor comparison page.

Ask how the platform models a product that is configured rather than picked from a list. Ask what happens when the price depends on the customer rather than the product. Ask how returns, partial shipments and cancellations flow back into finance. Ask what the upgrade path looks like when the platform releases a major version, and who is responsible for it.

Then ask each candidate to demonstrate your two most awkward requirements rather than a generic storefront. The demonstration you are shown by default is always the one that works well.
