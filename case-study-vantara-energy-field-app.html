Vantara Energy · Energy & Utilities

Field inspection software for places with no signal

1,900 field engineers, 40,000 assets, and connectivity that could not be assumed. An offline-first inspection platform that works in a substation basement.

The challenge

Vantara's field engineers inspected 40,000 distribution assets on paper forms, transcribed later into a central system by a back-office team of eleven. Median lag between inspection and record was nine days; roughly 4 percent of forms were never reconciled at all.

A previous mobile attempt had failed for a straightforward reason: it required connectivity. Substations, culverts and rural sites routinely have none, and an application that stalls at the point of use is worse than paper.

Regulatory reporting depended on this data, which made the reconciliation gap a compliance exposure as well as an efficiency problem.

Our approach

We treated offline as the default operating state rather than a degraded mode. Every feature was specified twice — once for connected, once for disconnected — and the disconnected specification was the one that governed.

Our QA lead and two engineers spent a week shadowing inspections across five regions before test design started. That produced the conditions the test suite was eventually built around: gloved hands, direct sunlight, 4 percent battery, a device dropped in the wet, and a sync attempt interrupted mid-flight.

Conflict resolution was designed with the inspectors rather than for them. Where two records disagree, the system presents both with provenance and asks a human — an approach that has kept the conflict rate at 0.3 percent and, more importantly, kept trust in the data.

What we built

A Flutter application with a local-first data layer, deterministic sync with vector-clock conflict detection, and full media capture — photographs, annotations, voice notes — queued and uploaded opportunistically.

Dynamic inspection forms driven by an asset-type schema, so Vantara's own engineering standards team changes forms without a release cycle.

A device-lab test suite running against fourteen physical handset configurations, plus automated network-condition simulation covering complete loss, high latency and partial packet loss during sync.

Results

Median time per inspection fell 52 percent, largely by eliminating transcription and re-visits for missing data.

The record lag went from nine days to under four minutes for connected sites and to the next signal window for the rest. Unreconciled inspections went to zero.

The back-office transcription team of eleven was redeployed to asset analysis — a redeployment Vantara planned for at the outset and communicated early, which is the main reason the rollout met no resistance.

Their test engineers spent a week in the field with our inspectors before writing a single test case. It shows in what the product does not do — there is nothing in it that assumes a good day.— Head of Asset Management, Vantara Energy
